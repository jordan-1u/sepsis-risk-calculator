SENTRY-Africa: Methods and Sources

Last updated: October 20, 2026

This page documents how the SENTRY-Africa app calculates each result, and the published sources behind it. The app is a reference aid for clinicians. It is not a diagnostic device and does not replace clinical judgment.

Universal Vital Assessment (UVA) score

The UVA score and its risk categories come from Moore et al. [1]. Points are added for each variable:

VariableCriterionPoints
Temperature<36 °C2
Heart rate≥120 bpm1
Respiratory rate≥30 breaths/min1
Systolic BP<90 mm Hg1
SpO₂<92%2
Glasgow Coma Scale [3]<154
HIV infectionYes2

Totals of 0–1 are low risk, 2–4 intermediate, and ≥5 high [1].

Lactate and UVA score trajectories

Point-of-care lactate predicts sepsis mortality in Uganda [2]. In the SENTRY-Africa cohort (adults with sepsis in Mbarara, Uganda), lactate and UVA score were measured at 0, 6, 12, 18 and 24 hours after admission. Trajectory groups were identified with latent class mixed models [4]:

  • UVA score: Class 1 high, decreasing; Class 2 high, stable.
  • Lactate: Class 1 low, stable; Class 2 high, decreasing; Class 3 increasing.

In the app, the user chooses the class that best matches the patient's serial values. The app does not assign classes automatically.

14-day mortality model

Predicted 14-day mortality comes from a logistic regression fitted in the SENTRY-Africa cohort (n = 155 with complete data):

logit(p) = −3.45938 + 0.42977 × baseline UVA + 1.40085 × [UVA class 2] + 1.31142 × [lactate class 2] + 2.80731 × [lactate class 3] − 0.53399 × IV fluids (L)

Apparent AUC 0.824 (95% CI 0.756–0.891). The app shows results below 10% as low risk, 10–40% as intermediate, and 40% or higher as high.

Limitations

The model was derived in a single cohort and has not been externally validated or peer reviewed. Its AUC was measured in the derivation cohort, so performance elsewhere may be lower. The negative coefficient for IV fluids reflects patterns in the cohort and is not a treatment recommendation.
